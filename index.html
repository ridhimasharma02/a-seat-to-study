INT335 · DESIGN THINKING · P06

Built for the way students study.

A student-centered response to campus study-space search friction.

Why this exists

Student research for the A Seat to Study project identified noisy hostel rooms, unattended belongings blocking desks, limited functional charging points, and a lack of pre-departure vacancy information.

Research foundation

30 student interviews and 23 survey responses informed the project's empathy map, user persona, and selected How Might We question: How might we help students find a free, quiet seat without walking around looking for one?

What this prototype demonstrates

Interactive floor selection, seat-level availability, filtering, reservations, check-in, automatic hold expiry, and visual occupancy reporting. All data is simulated and stored in this browser; no real seat is booked at LPU.

Proposed next steps

Validate the actual floor plan and power points, obtain campus permission, integrate trusted occupancy updates, test fair seat-release rules, and conduct a small reading-hall pilot.
